How I build

Built with AI.
Checked against the source.

I'm a classroom teacher, not a software team. I build these tools with an AI coding assistant (Claude Code), and I'm open about which part is mine and which part is the AI's.

What I do

Decide what teachers need. Describe each problem in teacher language. Check every output against TEA's published documents. Push back when a fix misses the real cause.

What the AI does

Writes and rewrites the code. Keeps a decisions log of every design choice and bug. Writes the automated tests.

What the AI never does

Decide what teachers need. See student data. It sees code and column headers only, never a student row.

1

Fix the start and the end with synthetic data

Invented names and scores give a fixed "before" and "after," so the target stops moving.

2

Know where north is

Check against what TEA actually published, not what you or the AI assume.

3

Insist on root cause

A patch that hides the symptom is a bug you'll meet again.

4

Build it with people

Teachers and administrators have to shape the tool, or they won't use it.

Campus PD day · Midlothian ISD · October 2026

Grade Faster with Rubrics in SpeedGrader

A Canvas PD package for a middle school staff with a wide range of Canvas experience. One goal: be a resource and don't waste teachers' time.

  • Needs assessment first. A two-minute anonymous survey decided which sessions to build, written so no teacher felt called out.
  • One outcome per session. A 15–20 minute "I do / you do" session where every teacher leaves having graded a student with a rubric.
  • A safety one-pager. Canvas-to-Skyward Grade Sync overwrites Skyward every night; one page shows teachers how not to lose hand-entered grades.
  • A tested AI help assistant. A Gemini Gem that answers only from the PD materials, checked against a 12-question test set until every answer passed.
  • Built for the teacher who missed it. Self-teaching slides, plain-language step guides, and one "Start Here" folder.
